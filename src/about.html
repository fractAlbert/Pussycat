About

The maker

Pussycat was a brand of Breiter Walter GmbH & Co. KG, a plastics manufacturer in Schötmar, part of Bad Salzuflen in North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany. The company is listed in local directories under plastic products rather than toys, which fits the puzzles themselves: thin moulded plastic trays with printed sliding tiles, cheap to produce and sold as novelties and gifts.

Puzzles carry Pussycat and Made in Germany on the back, often with an article number such as 80 2971 or 802931. Some later Escher pieces are marked Holland as well as Germany.

What they made

The catalog is broader than the art series. Alongside the paintings there are crossword designs, children's motifs, and promotional puzzles printed for companies — a Lufthansa apple among them. The sliding puzzle was the house format, and almost anything could be printed on it.

The art series

The best-known line reproduces well-known paintings and prints. Artists seen so far include M.C. Escher, Wassily Kandinsky, Gustav Klimt, Vincent van Gogh, Pablo Picasso, René Magritte, Joan Miró, August Macke, and the contemporary German painter Otmar Alt.

The Escher puzzles are the most commonly found today. Listings describe them as produced for the Escher heirs, dated around 1990, with the original print reproduced on the back of the packaging. Typical dimensions are about 15 × 12.5 cm.

Formats

Two layouts turn up. Most art puzzles are described as 49 tiles in a 7×7 arrangement — the image is complete, and a spare cell in one corner is what lets the tiles move at all. Others follow the classic layout, where the blank is a missing tile inside the picture: the Puddle puzzle is listed as 55 sliding pieces plus a place holder.

Dates

Examples are offered as 1970s through 1990s, with individual puzzles dated 1990, 1991, and 1996. Directory entries for the company suggest it did not continue past around 2000.

About this catalog

Entries are compiled from sale listings and auction archives. Anything not yet checked against the physical puzzle is marked as such in its detail view, and each entry links to the listing it came from. Tile counts, article numbers, and titles taken from listings are frequently approximate — sellers describe the same puzzle several different ways.

Corrections are welcome, particularly from anyone holding these in hand.
