About the images

Where the photographs come from

Most photographs here were taken by other people — sellers photographing a puzzle for an auction or shop listing. They are used as placeholders: a catalog entry with no picture is hard to recognise, and a seller's photograph is often the only surviving record that a particular design existed at all.

Each entry links back to the listing its photographs came from. Where a photograph is credited, the credit belongs to whoever took it, not to this catalog.

Why the files are stored here

The images are copied into this site rather than linked from their original location. This is not an attempt to pass them off as our own — it is the only way a catalog can work. Listings are taken down when items sell, and a linked image simply disappears, usually within weeks. A reference that quietly empties itself is worse than no reference.

Replacing placeholders

Every borrowed photograph is temporary. As puzzles are acquired and photographed directly, the placeholder is replaced with an original picture and the entry is marked as verified. The long-term goal is a catalog whose images are entirely its own.

If you want a photograph removed

If you took one of these photographs and would rather it were not here, it will be removed — no argument, no justification needed. The entry stays, the picture goes.

Contact: an address for removal requests has not been set up yet. Until it is, please open an issue on the project repository.

The artworks themselves

Many puzzles reproduce paintings and prints whose rights sit with the artists' estates — the Escher puzzles were made under licence for the Escher heirs, and Cordon Art is credited on some of them. Photographs of the puzzles are catalogued here as manufactured objects. Nothing on this site is a reproduction of an artwork offered for its own sake.
